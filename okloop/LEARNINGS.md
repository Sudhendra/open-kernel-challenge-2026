### DEAD absorb-probe1 (Nov 1, algebraic absorption PO = P*diag(beta1*pre)*O, RO likewise): the s0-only pooler+router stream cannot be hidden under attention
- Idea: u-part GEMV (alpha1*s0*pre, depends on s0 only) streams before/during attention; after attention only O u + PO u + RO u remain (PO fp32 hi/lo 16.8 MB + RO 1 MB beside the 12.6 MB compressed O). - Why (nsys, 12 replays): sk2f does start beside attention (12.1 us vs attention 9.3-17.1 at d1s) but runs 21.8 us (d1s) / 29.0 us (d1f) against 19.5 us alone: it is decode/issue-bound at ~3 TB/s, not HBM-bound, and shares SMs with attention (attention d1f 14.5 -> 15.6 us). The o stage then follows it (3.9 us incremental) instead of preceding it: chain end 38.1 vs 39.2 us (d1s), 45.1 vs 46.5 us (d1f). HBM idle time under attention is real (5-12 us) but this kernel cannot consume it.
- Cost side (analytic, not built): the o stage would read 30 MB (12.6 compressed O + 16.8 fp32 PO + 1 RO, none compressible) instead of 12.6 MB: +17 MB at the 3.6-3.8 TB/s sk2 rate = +4.5 us (bf16 PO: +2.5 us). Net is -3 us to 0, i.e. slower or tie at d1s/d1f, and (a) putting the s0 rows into the qkv launch nets the same 3.6 us of removed stage overhead against 4.2 us of added bytes.
- Not tried (needs a different consumer, not this stack): a pre-wait stream kernel that is truly HBM-bound (plain LDG/TMA + FMA on bf16-raw P, 64 MB) could use the idle window, but it reads 16 MB more than the compressed stream. ### DEAD codec11 (Nov 1, 11-bit lossless dense BF16 weight codec: bit-plane, 3-bit exponent window + escapes; muse_bf16_proj/experiments/codec11/, EXP-069)
- Idea: weights are bf16(randn/sqrt(K)), entropy 10.46 b/w vs the shipped 12; an 11-bit format would cut dense bytes 8.3%. Format: raw byte plane (e0|m7) + 3-bit code, window of 8 exponents; escapes (weights below the window) are 1.2-1.35% of weights.
